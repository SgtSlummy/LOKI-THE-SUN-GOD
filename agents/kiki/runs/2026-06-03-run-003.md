## Key Learning Notes

- Two-font systems work best when the headline carries personality and the body font stays quiet.
- For short poster tasks, a large type family matters less than a clear role split and readable lines.
- A limited typographic scale keeps the layout calmer and makes the hierarchy easier to scan.
- If the type feels busy, remove styles before adding more.

## Studio Process Principles

- Define the voice of the typography first, then keep the system disciplined enough that the voice stays readable.
- Use a limited typographic scale that fits the layout and avoids visual clutter.

## Beginner Assignment

### Title

Two-Font Poster Study

### Learning Objective

Pair one expressive headline font with one readable body font so a simple poster has clear hierarchy without extra clutter.

### Estimated Time

30-45 minutes.

### Materials / Tools

- Figma Free or Canva Free
- Google Fonts, or Figma's free Google Fonts picker
- Optional contrast checker
- Notes app or text editor

### Design Brief

Design a text-only poster for a fictional free workshop called "Poster Basics Night." Include a title, one short description, the date and time, the location, and a simple call to action. Make the piece feel clear, friendly, and easy to scan.

### Steps

1. Pick exactly two font families from Google Fonts or Figma's font picker: one expressive font for the title and one simple font for everything else.
2. Set up a single-page layout, then place the title, description, event details, and call to action in a simple order.
3. Build the hierarchy using size, weight, case, and spacing before adding any decorative elements.
4. Refine the line breaks and alignment so the poster reads quickly in one glance.
5. Export the poster and write a short note explaining why the font pair works for this brief.

### Constraints

- Use exactly two font families.
- Use no images, textures, or decorative illustrations.
- Keep the content to no more than four text blocks.
- Keep body copy short enough to read comfortably at a glance.
- Use one alignment strategy consistently across the piece.

### Deliverables

- One final poster export as PNG or PDF.
- One short rationale naming the fonts used and why they fit together.

### Reflection Questions

- Which font is doing most of the work, and does it do that job well?
- If you swapped the headline and body fonts, would the message still feel clear?

### Optional Stretch Challenge

Make a second version that keeps the same layout but swaps one font family, then compare the mood and readability of the two versions.

## Critique Rubric

| Criterion | 1 - Needs Work | 2 - Developing | 3 - Solid Beginner Attempt | 4 - Strong Beginner Work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Font role separation | The fonts blur together or fight for attention. | The fonts are different, but the roles are still unclear. | The headline and body fonts have mostly clear jobs. | Each font has a distinct role that supports the message well. |
| Hierarchy | The viewer cannot tell what matters first. | The order is partly clear, but some emphasis feels off. | The main information reads in a mostly sensible order. | The hierarchy is immediate and easy to follow. |
| Readability | Text feels cramped, awkward, or hard to read. | Readability works in some places but not others. | The text is readable and mostly comfortable to scan. | The text is consistently easy to read at a glance. |
| Consistency and restraint | Too many styles or effects make the poster noisy. | The design is somewhat controlled but still a little busy. | The design stays mostly restrained and focused. | The design feels disciplined and intentional. |
| Alignment and spacing | Edges and gaps feel random or unstable. | Some alignment exists, but the structure is uneven. | The layout has a clear alignment system and usable spacing. | Alignment and spacing make the page feel calm and organized. |
